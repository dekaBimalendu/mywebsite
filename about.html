About

A scientist who uses engineering as a tool.

I'm Bimal, an MSc Biomedical Engineering graduate from the University of Twente (August 2026), currently applying for PhD positions in biomedical engineering and tissue biology across the Netherlands and Europe.

What I work on

My background is a hybrid one: engineering frameworks — microfluidics, computational modeling, closed-loop systems — applied to biological questions, specifically iPSC-derived cell models, circadian biology, and neurovascular research. I don't think of myself as an engineer who happens to do biology; the engineering is in service of a biological question, not the other way around.

That question, most often, is about the limits of animal models. Rodent models are the default in much of preclinical research, but they frequently fail to capture human-relevant physiology — differences in metabolism, circadian machinery, and tissue architecture that matter for translation. Human-relevant in vitro systems — organ-on-chip platforms, iPSC-derived co-cultures — are one way to close that gap, and they're the throughline across my research so far.

How I got here

During my MSc at Twente, I designed a programmable microfluidic platform for reproducing coronary bypass graft oxygen profiles in cardiac tissue — a simulation and modelling-based proposal for a closed-loop oxygen modulation module, built around hiPSC-derived cardiomyocyte culture. Alongside the modelling, I ran the wet-lab side: hiPSC-CM culture, LDH assays, and qPCR.

A research internship at the University of Oxford (Radcliffe Department of Medicine) took me further into circadian biology — entrainment and synchronization experiments in iPSC-derived neuron and primary human astrocyte co-cultures, using BMAL1::Firefly Luciferase reporter assays. I taught myself transfection from scratch there, going from no experience to independent protocol optimization in about a month, and ended up establishing the lab's first glutamate uptake assay for primary human astrocytes.

Before the MSc, I spent time in a medical device startup (MobiLab, IIT Guwahati), developing hardware toward FDA and CE-IVDR compliance. That experience shaped how I think about engineering problems generally — working under real constraints, with regulatory seriousness built in from the start rather than bolted on at the end.
